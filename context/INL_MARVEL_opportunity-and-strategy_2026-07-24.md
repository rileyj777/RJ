## 1. Executive summary and recommendation

MARVEL is INL's small (85 kWth) demonstration microreactor, purpose-built as a two-year test bed where private industry can validate microreactor applications [1][2]. INL is actively recruiting industry to run tests and demonstrations on it. The first Expression of Interest (EOI) round has already closed and five end-user teams were selected; the next formal entry point is a Notice of Opportunity (NO) expected in calendar year 2026 [4].

JE is not a nuclear operator and should not pretend to be a reactor "end user." JE's real, defensible role is as the **engineering and hardware partner to an end-user team**, building the **non-nuclear balance-of-plant** that every MARVEL demonstration requires: heat-rejection skids, secondary-loop hardware, microgrid integration equipment, process skids for nuclear-heat applications, test rigs, instrumentation, and the non-nuclear controls replica INL already uses [2]. That work is squarely JE's thermal, fluid, and test wheelhouse and stays clear of the fission boundary.

**Recommendation:** treat MARVEL as a relationship and teaming play, not a prime bid. If Andy clears the nuclear-adjacency, pursue two low-exposure paths in parallel: (a) partner with an already-selected end-user team as their hardware builder now, and (b) position JE (or a JE client) for the CY2026 NO. Use INL Industry Days (Nov 11-12) as the entry [6].

## 2. What MARVEL is (the asset)

- An 85 kWth microreactor producing more than 10 kWe of electricity in the first stage, with process heat above 300 C available to end users in the second stage [1].
- Fueled with TRIGA-type fuel (uranium enriched to 19.75% U-235); primary heat moved by natural circulation of NaK liquid metal through four intermediate heat exchangers to secondary GaInSn loops [2].
- Designed as a transportable, non-permanent test bed installed in INL's TREAT facility, remotely operated, with a roughly two-year operational window [2]. Reached 90% final design in September 2023 [2].
- Mission: give the private sector a platform to mature novel nuclear technologies and demonstrate new microreactor use cases [1].

## 3. Program status and timeline (the critical timing finding)

- The **first EOI round closed 08/30/2025** [1]. It is still listed on INL's vendor portal, but that first window has passed.
- INL has already **competitively selected five end-user teams** for the first experiments (reported participants include AWS and GE Vernova, with oil-and-gas majors; test cases include desalination, remote operations, and advanced sensors), with final agreements expected in 2026 [4].
- A **formal Notice of Opportunity (NO) is expected in CY2026**, following EOI review, with more detail on requirements, funding, and timeline [1][4].
- The engagement path after selection is two-step: a feasibility assessment (intended FY2026) followed by physical test and demonstration (intended FY2027-2028), subject to Congressional appropriation [1].

Bottom line: JE missed the first EOI, but the program is early and multi-year. The teaming door (with selected end users) is open now; the direct door (the CY2026 NO) is ahead.

## 4. The six utilization categories, and where JE-adjacent value sits

INL invited proposals in six categories [1]. JE's hardware value is highest in the bolded ones:

1. Novel nuclear-electricity applications (nuclear-powered microgrids, data centers).
2. **Novel nuclear-heat applications (chemical processing, synfuel production, novel power conversion)** -> the end user needs a process skid to take MARVEL's >300 C heat; that skid is JE work.
3. Data access (analyses, documentation, engineering drawings).
4. Operating the reactor in specific modes to validate models.
5. **Advanced instrumentation and control (semi-autonomous, far-field remote operations)** -> sensor and control hardware, test fixtures.
6. Novel safeguards and security paradigms.

Across all of them, the physical enablers are non-nuclear: heat-rejection equipment and microgrid components installed in portable trailers outside the reactor building, secondary-loop hardware, and MACS, the non-nuclear replica of MARVEL used for controls testing [2]. This is the cleanest JE entry: build the surrounding hardware, not the reactor.

## 5. JE's role and the core-IP / compliance line

JE builds the hardware around the science, never the fissile core. Concretely, JE can offer an end-user team:

- Process and thermal skids that use MARVEL's electricity or >300 C heat (heat exchangers, reactors, power-conversion hardware) [1].
- Heat-rejection and secondary-loop hardware, and microgrid or battery-integration equipment for the trailer-mounted balance-of-plant [2].
- Test rigs, fixtures, and instrumentation packages; support on the non-nuclear MACS controls replica [2].
- JE's analysis-driven design (thermal, fluid, structural CFD and FEA), fabrication, and test, delivered end to end.

What JE does not touch: the reactor core, the fuel, the primary NaK boundary, or anything inside the nuclear licensing envelope. That stays with INL and the nuclear-qualified team members.

## 6. Compliance gate (read before any outbound step)

MARVEL is a fission reactor with enriched uranium fuel [2]. Under JE's standard compliance rule, nuclear and fuel-cycle work routes to Andy for an explicit go/no-go. Nothing goes to INL, and no teaming commitment is made, until Andy clears it. Even in the clean balance-of-plant framing above, the association is nuclear and the decision is his.

## 7. Strategy and recommended play

If Andy clears the gate, run two paths in parallel:

- **Path A, teaming now (faster, lower exposure).** Approach one or more of the selected end-user teams as their hardware and engineering partner for the FY26 feasibility and FY27-28 build phases. JE builds their non-nuclear demonstration hardware. This needs no EOI and starts a revenue relationship sooner.
- **Path B, position for the CY2026 NO.** Get JE on INL's radar as the go-to build partner for microreactor demonstrations so that when the NO drops, JE is named in multiple responders' plans, or supports a JE client that responds.
- **Entry point for both:** INL Industry Days, Nov 11-12, Idaho Falls [6]. Program contact for MARVEL utilization is Stacie Strain [1]. Relationship first, capability statement second.

## 9. Risks

- **Compliance and reputational:** nuclear association requires Andy's sign-off and careful scoping to the non-nuclear boundary (Section 6).
- **We missed the first EOI:** the fastest path is now teaming with an already-selected end user rather than a prime response [1][4].
- **JE is not the end user:** JE needs a partner (an end-user team or a JE client) to have a seat; JE does not hold a use case of its own for the reactor.
- **Appropriations risk:** the physical demonstration phase is subject to Congressional appropriation, so timelines can slip [1].
- **Short window:** MARVEL's operational life is about two years, so hardware must be ready early [2].
