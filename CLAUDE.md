## Sourcing and citations (every deliverable)

Cite sources on everything produced for Riley, for visible credibility with Andy, JE leadership,
and funding partners. End every report, brief, memo, analysis, or proposal with a visible
**Sources / References** section (web URLs with the date verified, internal files/PDFs, patent or
PCT numbers, monday items, CRM records, notable tool outputs), and attribute specific facts,
figures, and dates inline. Distinguish verified (source + date) from estimated, and flag anything
unverified. Exception: outbound copy (cold emails, LinkedIn) carries credibility as named specifics
inline, not a bibliography. Mirrors the funding skill's "every fact carries a date and a source"
bar; see auto-memory `cite-sources-on-deliverables`.

## Deadlines on opportunity deliverables

Always surface deadlines prominently: a **Key dates** block near the top of any opportunity brief,
one-pager, exec summary, or board item, never buried in prose. State each date with its year and
whether it is upcoming or past (never let a bare "August 30" read as the current year), note what it
gates (EOI, LOI, Notice of Opportunity, full proposal, Q&A) and any noon-ET cutoff, and distinguish
confirmed (source + date) from expected/TBD. See auto-memory `always-surface-deadlines`.

### Pricing standards
- T&M rate: $205/hr
- Materials/subcontractor markup: 25%
- WA sales tax: 9.1%
- T&M accuracy: ±20% assuming no scope change
- Fixed-Fee = T&M × 1.25
- Always present both T&M and Fixed-Fee; recommend Fixed-Fee as the budgetary target

### Billing model
Retainer-based. Services pre-paid in monthly reserved hours. Unused hours roll over. Each
invoice cycle: current balance + recommended future purchases. Invoices due on receipt. On
completion, remaining hours roll to future projects or are refunded at client's discretion.
Invoices itemize labor (date/category/description/hours) and materials (date/description/cost).

### Mandatory exclusions — never include without explicit instruction
- Prior art, patent review, patent search, literature review, background research, or surveys
  of existing approaches — omit entirely, do not substitute alternative phrasing.
- Helium leak testing — JE does not own a mass spectrometer; use pressure-decay testing as the
  default instead.

### Exclusions section template
Open with: *"The following items are assumed to be out of scope for this phase. These
exclusions can be provided at our normal T&M rates if desired."* Standard exclusions:
long-duration endurance testing, on-site install/commissioning, long-term maintenance
agreements, site permitting/AHJ reviews, full CFD (when not in analysis scope), client-handled
items, placeholder items pending client spec. Close with: *"Any services not expressly
described in this proposal."*

### IP Rights section template
*"Johnston Engineering will assign all intellectual property related to this project to
[Client]. Johnston Engineering is willing to assist [Client] and patent attorneys with
IP-related communications such as transferring IP, generating patent figures, and similar
activities, to be billed at our normal T&M rates. We have not budgeted any IP-related tasks in
this proposal."*
JE supports the client's patent attorneys only — JE does not perform patent work itself.

### Project Timeline section
State duration in months + total labor hours. List labor categories (typically: Mech engineer,
Mech designer, Electrical engineer, Software engineer, Electrical tech). State staffing and
lead-time assumptions. Give a delivery date estimate from an assumed kickoff. Include an
accelerated-schedule paragraph: adding personnel can shorten timeline but causes ~5–10% labor
cost increase for ~30% reduction in delivery time due to coordination overhead.

### Deliverables section (two subsections)
- *Design Documentation:* 3D CAD (SolidWorks/STEP), fab & assembly drawings (SolidWorks/PDF),
  updated P&ID + control docs, electrical schematics + control-panel drawings, design calcs +
  equipment sizing, BOM, calibration certs for calibrated instruments, initial + final test data.
- *Physical Deliverable:* Fully assembled and verified system, ready for shipment (typically one
  bullet).

## Key judgment calls to preserve

- **Pricing source discipline:** use detail-tab totals from the SOW costing spreadsheet, not the
  MGT roll-up tab, which silently excludes Testing labor rows.
- **Onsite assembly cost logic:** shipping-container savings are modest; travel and onsite labor
  inefficiency dominate the cost delta. Client self-assembly is usually the meaningful cost
  reduction lever, not the container/packaging choice.
- **Technical accuracy matters:** e.g., ammonia detector placement — NH₃ is lighter than air
  (MW 17 vs. 29 for air), so detectors should be at breathing-zone height for personnel
  exposure, not low-mounted. Catch and correct errors like this on sight; carry the correction
  into future work.
- **"X% less" pricing instructions:** "25% less" means ÷1.25 (removing a safety-factor markup),
  not ×0.75. Flag explicitly which interpretation is being used whenever this type of
  instruction comes up.
